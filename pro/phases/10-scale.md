# Phase 10 — Scale and growth

**Purpose:** The founder's first repeatable growth engine. One channel that keeps working at a known cost, customers worth more than they cost, and a process someone else could run.
**Modules:** 07 traction and growth, 06 metrics, 03 business model, 08 founder strategy
**Entry condition:** A Phase 09 verdict of Yes or In one segment.
**Pass gate:** Sales stage conditions: 1 channel predictable for 3 consecutive months (rule of thumb), LTV at least 3x CAC (rule of thumb), payback 12 months or less (rule of thumb), and 1 customer won by someone other than the founder.
**Artifact:** `references/artifacts/growth-plan.md`, `references/artifacts/cac-ltv-model.md`
**Next phase:** back to the open loop, with the growth plan as the next review

## The questions

One question first. Suggest the area where the founder's own numbers look
weakest.

> Which is most in the way right now — the channel, the economics, the price,
> the process, or your own time?

On *In one segment*, every answer below stays inside that segment.

## 1. One repeatable channel

Start from the channel that already brought the best customers, by the
founder's own records — not the fashionable one. Run it as an experiment with
three things written down: a duration, a target number of customers, and a
spend cap.

No second channel until the first one repeats. Two half-run channels teach
nothing about either.

## 2. Unit economics, with the working shown

Use the founder's numbers. Label every input.

- **CAC** = (spend + tool costs for the period) divided by new customers in
  that period
- **LTV** = monthly revenue per customer times gross margin divided by monthly
  churn
- **Payback in months** = CAC divided by (monthly revenue times gross margin)
- **LTV to CAC** = LTV divided by CAC

LTV at least 3x CAC (rule of thumb); payback 12 months or less (rule of thumb),
ideally 6 or less for a self-funded founder (rule of thumb). Say the label out
loud every time; a founder who thinks 3x is a law treats 2.9x as a failure.

**Zero churn, or under 3 months of history: do not divide by it.** Say churn
cannot be measured yet. Either leave LTV as not measurable, or use a capped
24-month lifetime labelled `UNVERIFIED`. Never show an unbounded LTV.

**Founder time** stays out of CAC unless the founder wants it in — but say
once that founder-led selling hides the real cost.

A missing input gets asked for once. If it is still missing, the result is
marked `UNVERIFIED` with the assumption written beside it.

## 3. Price from real usage

Price against the value customers actually name, found in the survey's
main-benefit answers and in how they use it. Test changes on new customers
only. If nobody ever pushes back on price, it is probably too low (rule of thumb).

## 4. The sales playbook

Write down how the founder actually wins customers today, in enough detail
that someone else could follow it: lead source, first message, demo or
onboarding, close, follow-up. Use the founder's real wording. Mark any step
that is still improvised, because that is the step that will not transfer.

## 5. The first hire

- **Ask first:** can an automation handle this? A scheduled follow-up, an
  onboarding sequence, a booking link.
- **When:** a proven, repeatable task takes a large share of the founder's
  week.
- **Who:** the role that removes the biggest bottleneck. Usually a part-time
  contractor first.
- **How:** a small paid trial project before any longer commitment.
- Never recommend a hire for a channel or a process that has not repeated yet.

## When the gate is not met

**A founder Phase 09 turned away gets no plan here either.** Zero active users
is the first of the three sanctioned refusals in `references/fit-ladder.md`,
and it does not stop being one because the founder walked into this phase
instead of that one. Say what 09 says — there is nothing to measure yet — and
send them back to the loop. A growth plan for zero users is a channel answer
to a product question, and the stamp would not save it.

Warn-then-stamp below is for a founder whose signals are short, not for one who
has no users at all.

Below a Phase 09 verdict of Yes or In one segment, warn first: scaling before
people love the product mostly buys more people who leave. Then follow
warn-then-allow in `references/fit-ladder.md` and put the gap stamp at the top
of the growth plan.

Say which parts of the Sales condition are met and which are not, in numbers.
A founder with LTV at 2x CAC does not need encouragement; they need to know
which of the two numbers to move and which one action moves it.

Save `references/artifacts/growth-plan.md` as a 90-day plan and append dated
rows to `references/state-file.md`.
