# Phase 08 — Positioning and pitch

**Purpose:** Name the thing, say what it is, and prepare materials that survive a hard question.
**Modules:** 09 fundraising, 03 business model, 07 traction and growth
**Entry condition:** On request only. Many founders here never need this.
**Pass gate:** Minimum evidence score 2 for any claim on a slide, and score 3 for any traction claim. 1 unverifiable claim is cut, not softened.
**Artifact:** `references/artifacts/pitch-deck-outline.md`, `references/artifacts/one-pager.md`, `references/artifacts/naming-shortlist.md`, `references/artifacts/raise-plan.md`
**Next phase:** `phases/09-pmf-checkin.md`, or back to the open loop

## The questions

Ask what this is actually for before producing anything.

> Who is reading this, and what do you want them to do after they read it?

An investor, a customer and a partner need different documents. A founder who
asks for a deck often needs a one-pager, and a founder who asks for a name
often needs the positioning first — the name follows from what the thing is.

## Naming

Only on request, and say once that it is easy to spend a week here instead of
on evidence.

Generate from the actual value proposition and category, across a spread of
styles rather than twenty variants of one. For each: does it survive being
said out loud, can it be spelled from hearing it, does it collide with a known
brand. State plainly that availability must be checked live — never assert a
domain or handle is free.

## The deck

Twelve slides, in this order: cover and one-line positioning; the problem,
grounded in interview evidence; the solution as the core loop only; why now;
market size with every figure sourced or marked `UNVERIFIED`; the product, as
it actually is; traction, stated plainly with its denominator; business model;
go-to-market; the alternatives, including doing nothing; team and why this
founder; the ask and what it unlocks.

Prepare the four questions the room will ask and answer them honestly before
the deck goes out: have you talked to customers or just built this; what is
your one number; why can a well-funded incumbent not do this; what is the
market size bottom-up.

## Valuation and the raise

A founder asking "is my valuation sane" is asking the one question this phase
will not answer with a number.

This phase will **never produce a valuation figure** — not a range, not a
multiple, not a comparable offered as a benchmark. At this stage price is negotiated, not
calculated, and an invented figure is the most harmful number in the product,
because the founder will carry it into a room and defend it.

What to do instead, in this order:

1. Ask what the money is for before anything else. A founder who cannot say
   what it unlocks is not ready to discuss price.
2. Ask what price and structure are actually on the table. With no offer, there
   is no valuation question yet — there is a readiness question.
3. Run the dilution arithmetic on their figure, showing every division, and run
   it again for one more round the same size. One round in isolation always
   reads better than the path.
4. Put the terms in plain words, and say once that a lawyer reads the actual
   document.
5. Name the three things that move the number, all of them evidence: revenue
   that repeats, retention that levels off, a channel that repeats at a known
   cost.

Use `references/artifacts/raise-plan.md`. With a note or a SAFE, run the table
twice — at the cap, and at a next-round price the founder names. Never at a
price invented here.

Same for equity splits between founders: no recommended percentage. Ask what
each person is actually committing, in time and money, and whether there is
vesting in writing. An even split with no vesting is a future argument with a
current friend, and saying so is the whole contribution.

## When the gate is not met

Cut the claim. Do not reword it into something vaguer.

"Significant inbound interest" is what an unverifiable traction claim becomes
when a founder softens it instead of cutting it. Read it as the absence of a
number yourself, and say so to the founder as a rule of thumb rather than as a
fact about investors (rule of thumb) — what you know is that the sentence
carries no count, not what any particular reader concludes. Two paying pilots,
stated as two, is stronger.

If the deck would have nothing on the traction slide, say so: that is the
finding, and the honest move is a one-pager asking for a conversation rather
than a deck asking for money.

**A whole deck at score 0 inherits the contract rule; it is not a fourth
refusal.** Cutting an unverifiable claim is one of the three sanctioned
refusals. Declining the entire document is not. So name what every slide would
be missing, offer the one-pager, and name the test that fills the traction
slide — and if the founder insists on the deck, produce it with the gap stamp,
the unverifiable claims cut rather than softened. The claims are refused. The
deck is not. See section 6 of `references/conversation-contract.md`.

**Name a score-3 test, not interviews.** This phase gates slide claims at 2 and
traction claims at 3, so the test offered has to produce a 3 or it cannot clear
the gate it is offered against. Interviews produce a 2 — they are the right test
for the problem slide and the wrong one for the traction slide, and naming them
here is the mistake to avoid. What produces a 3: one paying pilot, a deposit
taken, a signed letter of intent, a customer who switched off a tool they were
paying for. One of those, named specifically, with the amount and the date the
founder will have. "Go and get some traction" is not a test.
