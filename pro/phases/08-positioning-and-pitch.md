# Phase 08 — Positioning and pitch

**Purpose:** Name the thing, say what it is, and prepare materials that survive a hard question.
**Modules:** 09 fundraising, 03 business model, 07 traction and growth
**Entry condition:** On request only. Many founders here never need this.
**Pass gate:** Minimum evidence score 2 for any claim on a slide, and score 3 for any traction claim. 1 unverifiable claim is cut, not softened.
**Artifact:** `references/artifacts/pitch-deck-outline.md`, `references/artifacts/one-pager.md`, `references/artifacts/naming-shortlist.md`
**Next phase:** `phases/09-pmf-checkin.md`, or back to the open loop

## The questions

Ask what this is actually for before producing anything.

> Who is reading this, and what do you want them to do after they read it?

An investor, a customer and a partner need different documents. A founder who
asks for a deck often needs a one-pager, and a founder who asks for a name
often needs the positioning first — the name follows from what the thing is.

## Naming

Only on request, and say once that it is easy to spend a week here instead of
on evidence.

Generate from the actual value proposition and category, across a spread of
styles rather than twenty variants of one. For each: does it survive being
said out loud, can it be spelled from hearing it, does it collide with a known
brand. State plainly that availability must be checked live — never assert a
domain or handle is free.

## The deck

Twelve slides, in this order: cover and one-line positioning; the problem,
grounded in interview evidence; the solution as the core loop only; why now;
market size with every figure sourced or marked `UNVERIFIED`; the product, as
it actually is; traction, stated plainly with its denominator; business model;
go-to-market; the alternatives, including doing nothing; team and why this
founder; the ask and what it unlocks.

Prepare the four questions the room will ask and answer them honestly before
the deck goes out: have you talked to customers or just built this; what is
your one number; why can a well-funded incumbent not do this; what is the
market size bottom-up.

## When the gate is not met

Cut the claim. Do not reword it into something vaguer.

"Significant inbound interest" is what an unverifiable traction claim becomes
when a founder softens it instead of cutting it, and every investor reads it
as the absence of a number. Two paying pilots, stated as two, is stronger.

If the deck would have nothing on the traction slide, say so: that is the
finding, and the honest move is a one-pager asking for a conversation rather
than a deck asking for money.
