# Phase 07 — Launch

**Purpose:** Get the product in front of the people already interviewed, through one channel, with one metric being watched.
**Modules:** 07 traction and growth, 06 metrics, 03 business model
**Entry condition:** Something exists that a person can use end to end.
**Pass gate:** 1 person has completed the core loop without help. Below that, this is a product problem and not a channel problem.
**Artifact:** `references/artifacts/launch-plan.md`, `references/artifacts/positioning-statement.md`
**Next phase:** `phases/09-pmf-checkin.md` once there are real users

## The questions

One question to find the break point before choosing a channel.

> When someone arrives today, where do they stop?

The answer decides everything else:

- **Nobody finds it** — an acquisition problem. One channel, chosen from where
  this specific buyer already spends time. Not three.
- **They arrive and do not get value** — a **product problem**. Fix the first
  five minutes before spending anything on traffic. More arrivals just means
  more people leaving.
- **They get value once and never return** — also a **product problem**. Same
  rule.
- **They return but never pay** — pricing and packaging, not channels.
- **They pay but tell nobody** — the product works; now it is word of mouth.

## The launch

Sized for one person with no budget.

1. A page that states the positioning and asks for exactly one thing. One
   call to action, not three.
2. Direct messages to everyone already interviewed. They get first access.
   This is the highest-converting list the founder will ever have.
3. One post where this buyer actually is — a specific community, not
   everywhere.
4. A way to hear back from day one: a reply-to address, a form, a channel.
   Launching with no feedback path wastes the launch.
5. One number being watched for week one. At this stage it is almost always
   about whether people reach first value, not revenue.

Pricing anchors to what the current workaround already costs the customer,
taken from interview evidence — not to a competitor's public page. One or two
tiers, not a matrix. If the price is still a guess, mark it `UNVERIFIED`.

## When the gate is not met

Nobody has completed the core loop unaided. Say that plainly, name it as a
product problem, and give one action: sit with one person while they try it,
without helping, and write down where they stop.

A founder who insists on launching anyway gets the plan with the gap stamp,
per `references/fit-ladder.md`. But say the sentence first: a launch into a
loop nobody can finish spends the one list of warm contacts they had.
