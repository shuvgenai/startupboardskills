# Evidence rubric

Every claim a founder makes gets a score from 0 to 3. Phase gates read the
score, not how confident the founder sounded.

Score the claim, not the person. Write the score into the assumption ledger
next to the claim it belongs to.

| Score | Name | What counts | What does not |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Opinion | The founder's own belief, reasoning or prediction. Anything a customer said about the future. Praise of any kind. | Nothing here is evidence. It is a hypothesis waiting for a test. |
| 1 | Secondhand | An article, a report, a forum thread, a review, a competitor's public claim. Someone agreeing in conversation without describing their own case. | Does not become primary because several sources repeat it. |
| 2 | Primary | An interview where the person described a **past behaviour** in their own recent experience: what they did, when, what it cost them, what they used instead. | A hypothetical. An agreement. A described intention. A conversation where you pitched first. |
| 3 | Behavioural | Someone paid. Someone signed up and used it. Someone booked time. Someone switched away from a tool. Someone referred another person unasked. | A waitlist email on its own. A verbal commitment with no date. A free trial never opened. |

## Four rules that decide most cases

**1. A compliment scores 0 no matter how many people gave it.**
"Ten people said they would use it" is one score-0 claim repeated ten times,
not a score-2 body of evidence. Say so to the founder plainly: that is
enthusiasm, and enthusiasm is free. Do not count it toward any interview
total.

**2. A hypothetical scores 0. Only a described past behaviour reaches 2.**
"Would you pay for this?" cannot produce evidence, whatever the answer. "What
did you do the last time this happened?" can.

**3. Score what was done, not what was felt.**
If the founder cannot say what the person actually did, the claim is not a 2.
Ask the one question that would settle it.

**4. The founder's own experience scores 0, however specific it is.**
A founder who was once this customer is describing the inside of the building.
Their own past behaviour is where the hypothesis came from, not evidence for
it — they already believed it, which is why they are building this.

This matters because it is the most convincing-sounding dodge there is. "I've
been a freelancer, I know the feeling" arrives with real detail attached and
still scores 0. Name it as the source of the idea, say plainly that it cannot
be counted, and ask how they will reach the first person who is not them.

The same applies to a co-founder, an employee, a family member, and anyone
the founder recruited from their own network: useful for designing the
question, never countable as the answer.

**It may shape what you ask. It may never be what you ask for.** Reaching for
the founder's own story to build a sharper question is right — they are the one
case whose detail you already have. Making it the turn's question is not: the
answer cannot move any gate, so the turn buys nothing while feeling like
progress. Ask it of someone who is not them.

**5. An event is behaviour. The reason given for it is testimony.**

They score separately, and collapsing them is how a 2 gets paid as a 3.

- The **event** — a sign-up, a purchase, a cancellation, a renewal, a switch,
  an arrival you counted — is behaviour. **Score 3**, when it is read from the
  product's own records rather than from someone's memory.
- The **reason** — "I found you through a friend", "I switched because of the
  price", "I cancelled because it got slow" — is that person's account of their
  own motive. **Score 2**, and a good 2: primary, specific, and still a story
  about why.

"40 users arrived last month" and "they arrived by word of mouth" are two claims
at two scores, even when one sentence carries both. Count the first, quote the
second, and never let the second inherit the first's score. This is the same
shape for every arrival, conversion and churn event, which is why it lives here
rather than in whichever module reaches it first.

## Worked examples, one per level

Take a founder building scheduling software for mobile dog groomers.

**Score 0.** "Groomers hate double bookings, it is obviously a huge problem."
The founder's own belief. Nothing outside their head.

**Score 0, disguised as better.** "I showed five groomers my mockup and four
said they would definitely sign up." Future tense, and a response to a pitch.
It is a compliment. Say so.

**Score 1.** "There is a thread in a grooming forum where people complain
about missed appointments." Real, outside the building, and still secondhand.
Nobody in that thread described their own week to you.

**Score 2.** "One groomer told me she lost two appointments last Thursday
because a client texted a change to her personal phone while she was mid-groom,
and she now writes every booking twice, once in her phone and once on a wall
calendar." A past event, with its own detail, and a workaround she already
pays for in time.

**Score 3.** "Two groomers paid a deposit for early access, and one of them
stopped renewing the paid calendar app she had been using." Money moved and a
tool was switched.

## How a gate uses the score

A phase gate names a minimum score and a count. Both must be met. Ten score-0
claims never satisfy a gate that asks for score 2.

When a gate is unmet, do not estimate the gap away. Say which score is missing
and name the one test that would produce it.
