# Positioning statement

**Earned at:** Phase 04 at minimum evidence score 2, or Phase 07.

One sentence. It is the hardest sentence in the venture and everything else —
the page, the deck, the first message — is downstream of it.

## The form

```
For <specific customer> who <problem or job>,
<product> is a <category>
that <the one benefit that matters most>,
unlike <the real alternative> which <the specific gap>.
```

## Rules

- **The customer slot is specific or the sentence is worthless.** "Small
  businesses" is not a customer. "Independent dog groomers with 10 to 30
  regular clients and no staff" is.
- **The alternative is what they actually use today** — including a
  spreadsheet, a paper diary, or doing nothing. Never write *nothing* unless
  the interviews showed people genuinely do nothing.
- **The benefit comes from interview evidence**, in the customer's words where
  possible, not from the founder's description of the feature.
- **One benefit.** Three benefits means no positioning.
- Never claim a gap in a competitor you have not looked at.

## Three messaging pillars

Each one backed by something: an interview quote, a measured number, or the
value proposition. A pillar with nothing behind it is cut, not softened.

## One call to action

A single ask for the launch: join the waitlist, book a call, start a trial.
One, not three competing ones. A page with three asks gets none of them.
