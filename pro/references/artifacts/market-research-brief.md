# Market research brief

**Earned at:** Phase 03. Score 1 for light, score 2 for medium, score 2 plus a
passed Problem stage for heavy.

Run with your web search and page-fetch tools. Pick the mode that matches what
is needed; never default to heavy.

## Light — the default

- Who else solves this today: 3 to 5 names, including the manual or
  do-nothing option.
- Whether this is a known category, a shrinking one, or not a category at all.
  Say which, and why that is good or bad here.
- One paragraph on which industry this actually belongs in.

## Medium

Everything in light, plus:

- **Competitors in depth**, 3 to 5: pricing model, target customer, visible
  differentiation, weakness visible from outside.
- **A positioning map** on two axes — price against specialisation, or
  self-serve against high-touch — showing where the gap is.
- **A buyer sketch** built around the trigger event that sends someone
  looking, not around demographics.

## Heavy — only above the gate

Everything in medium, plus:

- **Market sizing.** Every figure carries a source URL. Where no credible
  public figure exists, say so and build a bottom-up estimate instead —
  potential customers times a realistic price — with every assumption written
  out and marked `UNVERIFIED`.
- **Trend scan:** 2 or 3 recent developments, each cited.
- **Pyramid structure:** the one-sentence takeaway, then 3 or 4 supporting
  arguments, then the evidence under each, then the detail.

## Rules for every mode

- Never fabricate a figure. A number is sourced, or it is `UNVERIFIED`, or it
  does not appear.
- Favour primary sources — company sites, filings, regulator records — over
  aggregator blogspam.
- Paraphrase. Do not reproduce extended text from a source.
- A competitor count is a count of what you found, not of what exists. Say so.

Figures carried into other documents keep their source and as-of date, per
`## Numbers` in `references/state-file.md`.
