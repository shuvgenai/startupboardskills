# The fit ladder

Six stages. Use them to locate the founder honestly, not optimistically. Most
fresh ideas sit at Problem or Research. Say so plainly even when it is not what
the founder wants to hear.

Every number on this page is a **(rule of thumb)** and must be labelled as one
when you say it out loud. Adjust the bar out loud when the context demands it:
with only about 40 possible buyers in a niche, six interviews is a reasonable
bar, and saying why is part of the job.

Pass conditions count what people have **done**. See
`references/evidence-rubric.md`.

## 1. Problem

The problem exists, and people already spend time or money working around it.

**Pass condition:** about **10** interviews at score 2 or better (rule of
thumb); about **7 of 10** describe the problem from their own recent
experience without being prompted; about **5 of 10** already pay for a
workaround in money or time — a spreadsheet, a paper diary, another tool,
someone helping.
**Minimum evidence score:** 2

## 2. Research

How people solve this today is known, and the riskiest assumption is written
as something testable.

**Pass condition:** the **top 3** alternatives named, including the manual or
do-nothing option, and **1** riskiest assumption written as an if-then
hypothesis with a pass number attached.
**Minimum evidence score:** 1

## 3. Product

This specific product solves the problem for a small core group.

**Pass condition:** at least **5** target users complete the core loop without
help, and at least **3** make a real commitment — a pre-order, a deposit, a
signed letter of intent, a paid pilot. A waitlist email alone does not count.
**Minimum evidence score:** 3

## 4. Customer

The exact profile that gets the most value is known, and it sticks.

**Pass condition:** the founder can describe the **single** highest-value
customer profile; that group keeps using the product at its natural rhythm —
daily, weekly or monthly — for **4 or more weeks**; and at least **1** of them
referred someone without being asked.
**Minimum evidence score:** 3

## 5. Market

Demand pulls the product out of the founder rather than the founder pushing it.

**Pass condition:** a Phase 09 verdict of **Yes** (see
`phases/09-pmf-checkin.md`). A verdict of *In one segment* passes Market for
that segment only, and every later stage stays inside that segment.
**Minimum evidence score:** 3

## 6. Sales

Acquisition repeats, at a cost the business can carry.

**Pass condition:** one channel has delivered customers at a predictable cost
for **3 consecutive months**; **LTV at least 3x CAC** (rule of thumb); CAC
payback **12 months or less** (rule of thumb); and **someone other than the
founder** has won a customer using the playbook.
**Minimum evidence score:** 3

## When a pass condition is not met: warn, then allow

1. Say which condition is unmet and what evidence is missing, in numbers:
   "You have 4 interviews at score 2; the Problem condition asks for about 10."
2. Name the **one** action that closes the gap.
3. If the founder still wants to move ahead, help them — and put this line at
   the top of any document produced for a later stage:

   `> Built before <Stage> was passed. Missing: <evidence>.`

Never hide the gap, and never refuse. There is exactly one exception in the
whole product: a Phase 09 check-in with zero active users has nothing to
measure, so it is refused outright. See `phases/09-pmf-checkin.md`.

## Using this in a session

Name the stage the founder is actually at, then name the **one** unblocking
action for that stage. Not a to-do list across six stages. A founder at
Problem does not need a growth plan; they need five more conversations.

Count the evidence the founder has actually given you. If a piece has not come
up, ask for it — one question — rather than assuming it either way.
