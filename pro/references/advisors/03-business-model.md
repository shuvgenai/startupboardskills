# Business model

## When this module loads

- "How should I price this?"
- "How does it make money?"
- "I have users but no revenue"
- "What stops someone copying me?"
- Phases 01, 02, 04, and any session where a strong problem has no model
  behind it

## The lens

A business is a set of connected bets, and most of them are load-bearing. A
founder who can describe the product and the customer and nothing else has not
described a business; they have described two of nine boxes and left the rest
to hope.

The useful move is to lay the whole thing out fast, from guesses if necessary,
because a map of guesses tells you what to test next. The map is not the
achievement. It is a to-do list in disguise.

There is a real tension here worth surfacing to the founder in your own words.
Drawing the model early gives the founder something to defend rather than
something to learn from — the canvas becomes a position instead of a question.
Drawing it late means testing in the dark, with no sense of which answer would
matter. Say that out loud as a tradeoff, do not resolve it for them, and ask
which risk they would rather carry this month.

The question that does the most work: if one box is carrying the whole model,
which one, and why is it not being tested first?

## Questions to ask, in order

1. In one sentence, who pays, and for what?
2. Which single box in the model would sink the whole thing if it were wrong?
3. Why has that box not been tested yet?
4. What are people paying today to solve this, even partially?
5. What does it cost you to serve one customer for a month?
6. What is your unfair advantage — the thing a well-funded competitor could
   not simply copy next quarter?
7. If you have no unfair advantage yet, what would you have to build to get
   one?
8. Which revenue stream are you assuming, and which have you actually tried?

## Red flags in the answer

- "We'll figure out monetisation later." — Sometimes fine, usually a box
  nobody wants to look at. Ask which it is.
- "We'll execute faster than them." — Not an advantage. Everyone says it.
- "There's no competition." — There is always an alternative, including doing
  nothing. Not knowing it is the problem.
- "Our advantage is the technology." — Rarely durable alone. Ask what happens
  when it is copied.
- A price picked from a competitor's page with no reference to what the
  current workaround costs the customer.
- Nine boxes filled confidently with nothing behind any of them. A complete
  canvas is not a validated one.

## The evidence this module accepts

Scored against `references/evidence-rubric.md`. This module is trying to reach
**score 2** on the pricing and channel boxes, and **score 3** on willingness
to pay.

- **Score 2:** the founder can say what a specific customer currently spends,
  in money or hours, on the workaround.
- **Score 3:** someone has paid, pre-paid, or signed something.
- **Score 1:** competitor pricing read off a public page. Useful context, not
  validation.
- **Score 0:** a price derived from the founder's sense of what feels fair.

A box with no evidence behind it is marked `UNVERIFIED` on the canvas. An
honest canvas with six unverified boxes is more useful than a confident one.

## Output

`references/artifacts/lean-canvas.md` — nine blocks, every unevidenced block
marked `UNVERIFIED`, closing with the two or three boxes to test first.
