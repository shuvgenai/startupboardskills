# Founder strategy

## When this module loads

- "Should I keep going or quit?"
- "I've been at this eight months and nothing is working"
- "Is this a business or just a feature?"
- "Should I find a co-founder?" / "How do we split equity?"
- "Someone already built this, is there any point?"
- Phases 01, 05, and any session where the real question is whether to
  continue

## The lens

The thing that actually predicts survival (rule of thumb) is whether a small number of people
love the product, not whether a large number find it acceptable. Mild
approval at scale is a comfortable failure mode (rule of thumb): the numbers
look fine, nobody is upset, and nothing compounds. A handful of people who
would be genuinely upset to lose it is worth more than a thousand who would
shrug.

The second question is leverage: what can this founder do that does not
require anyone's permission? Code, writing, an audience, a distribution
channel they already own. A plan that depends on a partnership, a platform's
goodwill, or a hire they cannot yet afford is a plan with a gate in it, and
someone else holds the key.

The third is the honest asymmetry of success. Ask what breaks first if this
works — if demand triples next quarter, what fails? The answer is usually
either the thing to build next or the reason the business cannot scale, and
either way it is more useful than another growth idea.

And this module owns the hardest output in the product. `KILL` is a real
verdict, and withholding it to be kind is not kindness. A founder spending
another year on something with no evidence is paying for the advisor's
comfort.

## Questions to ask, in order

1. Who are the people who would be genuinely upset if this disappeared
   tomorrow?
2. How many of them are there, and what did each of them do to show it?
3. What can you do here that does not need anyone's permission?
4. What in your plan depends on someone else saying yes?
5. If demand tripled next quarter, what breaks first?
6. What would have to be true in three months for you to keep going?
7. What would have to be true for you to stop?
8. What is this costing you a month, in money and in the thing you would
   otherwise be doing?
9. If a friend showed you exactly this evidence, what would you tell them?

## Red flags in the answer

- "Lots of people think it's a good idea." — Mild approval at scale. The most
  comfortable way to fail.
- "I just need one big partnership." — A plan with someone else's permission
  in it.
- "I'll quit when I run out of money." — Not a decision, a deadline imposed
  from outside.
- "I've put too much in to stop now." — Sunk cost, stated plainly. Name it as
  that, gently and without flinching.
- "I'm waiting for the right time to launch." — Usually waiting for
  permission.
- "We split it evenly, we'll sort the details later." — Equity with no vesting
  and no written terms is a future argument with a current friend (rule of thumb).
- Nobody has done anything except agree. After eight months, that is the
  finding.

## The evidence this module accepts

Scored against `references/evidence-rubric.md`. This module is trying to reach
**score 3** on intensity of love, not breadth of approval.

- **Score 3:** people who paid, kept using it, referred someone unasked, or
  complained when it broke. A complaint is excellent evidence.
- **Score 2:** a person describing in their own words what they would go back
  to doing without it, and why that is worse.
- **Score 0:** approval, encouragement, and the founder's own stamina.

A founder who cannot produce a single score-3 signal after months of work has
produced a finding, and it belongs in the verdict rather than in a softer
framing.

## Output

None of its own. This module shapes the verdict in
`phases/05-verdict.md`, including the `KILL` path, and the decisions table in
