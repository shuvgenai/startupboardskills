# Problem discovery

## When this module loads

- "I have an idea for..." and nothing behind it yet
- "People really struggle with..."
- "Everyone I know has this problem"
- "I talked to some people and they liked it"
- Phases 00, 01, 02, 04

## The lens

A problem is only real if someone is already paying for it, in money, time or
embarrassment. Most ideas die because the founder validated the solution and
never checked the problem. The two conversations feel identical and only one of
them produces anything.

So the work is archaeological, not persuasive. You are looking for what the
person already did, last Tuesday, with a spreadsheet and a phone call,
because that is the only honest record of what they care about. Anything they
say about the future is a guess wearing the clothes of data.

Most importantly, a problem has a competitor even when no product exists.
Doing nothing is a competitor. A paper diary is a competitor. The question is
never "would you use this" but "what will you stop doing to make room for it",
and whether that thing is bad enough to fire.

## Questions to ask, in order

1. Walk me through the last time this happened to you.
2. What did you actually do about it?
3. What are you using today to handle it — a tool, a spreadsheet, a person, or
   nothing?
4. How much time or money does that cost you in a normal week?
5. What did you try before that, and why did you stop?
6. Where does this rank against the other things on your plate right now?
7. Who else in your world deals with this, and what do they do instead?
8. If this stayed exactly as it is for another year, what breaks?
9. Have you ever paid anyone to fix it, even badly?

## Red flags in the answer

- "They said they'd definitely use it." — A compliment. Score 0.
- "Everyone agrees it's a problem." — Agreement is not behaviour.
- "I'd pay for that, honestly." — Future tense from a person who has not.
- "I haven't talked to anyone yet but it's obvious." — Nothing outside the
  building.
- "I asked my friends and they loved the idea." — A friends-and-family signal,
  not a market signal.
- "They just live with it." — Possibly true, and possibly the whole answer:
  if nobody has ever tried to fix it, the pain may not be real.
- Describing the solution when asked about the problem. Catch it, name it, ask
  again.

## The evidence this module accepts

Scored against `references/evidence-rubric.md`. This module is trying to reach
**score 2** and, where money has moved, **score 3**.

- **Score 2:** an interview where the person described a specific recent
  instance — when it happened, what it cost, what they did instead — without
  being pitched first.
- **Score 3:** they already pay for a workaround, hired help, or switched tools
  to cope.
- **Score 0, however it is phrased:** interest, agreement, praise, or any
  statement about what someone would do.

Count only interviews where the person described their own experience. An
interview where the founder pitched is not an interview; it is a demo.
