# Product-market fit

## When this module loads

- "Do I have product-market fit?"
- "People sign up but nothing happens after that"
- "Is this good enough to push harder on?"
- "Should I add more features or get more users?"
- Phases 09, and Phase 02 whenever a founder with live users is being located
  on the ladder

## The lens

Fit is not a feeling and it is not a milestone you award yourself. It is the
moment the market starts pulling the product out of you faster than you can
push it, and the founder usually notices by being overwhelmed rather than by
being pleased.

Before that moment, the honest question is narrower: which underserved need
does this product actually hit, for whom, better than the alternative they
already have? A product that is slightly better for everyone is worse than a
product that is dramatically better for one narrow group (rule of thumb), because only the
second one produces people who would be upset if it vanished.

That gives the measurement its shape. Ask active users how they would feel if
they could no longer use it, and the share who say they would be very
disappointed tells you whether anything real is holding. The instrument is
called **the very-disappointed survey** throughout this product.

Fit also arrives in segments before it arrives overall. A figure below the bar
overall, with one customer type well above it, is not a failure — it is an
instruction about who to serve.

## Questions to ask, in order

1. What counts as an active user for this product, and how often would a real
   one use it?
2. Of your active users, what share would be very disappointed if they could
   no longer use it?
3. How many people answered that, and when?
4. Does that share differ by customer type?
5. What do the most disappointed users say the main benefit is, in their words?
6. What do the merely somewhat-disappointed users say is missing?
7. Which underserved need were you aiming at, and does their answer match it?
8. Are new users arriving without you pushing, and is that share growing?
9. Has anything changed in the last month that would move any of these numbers?

## Red flags in the answer

- "Everyone loves it." — Nobody measured anything. Score 0.
- "We have 2,000 signups." — Signups are not active users. Ask the definition
  again.
- "Retention is fine." — Fine is not a shape. Ask for the curve.
- "I surveyed everyone on the list." — The list is not the user base. The
  survey goes to active users only.
- "About 50%, I think." — An estimate standing in for a measurement. Never
  accept it; hand over the measurement kit.
- "We're pre-launch but I'm confident we have fit." — There is nothing to
  measure. Return them to their real stage.
- Numbers that cannot all be true at once. Say exactly what does not add up
  and ask one question.

## The evidence this module accepts

Scored against `references/evidence-rubric.md`. This module works at
**score 3** only — fit is measured from behaviour.

- **Score 3:** survey responses from identified active users; a retention
  table built from real sign-up and last-active dates; payment and
  cancellation records; a count of new users who arrived without being pushed.
- **Why they arrived is a separate claim at score 2**, per rule 5 of
  `references/evidence-rubric.md`. The arrival is counted; "word of mouth" is
  what a user said about why, and it does not inherit the count's score.
- **Score 2 at best:** what users told the founder in conversation about why
  they stay. Useful for the next product decision, not for the verdict.
- **Score 0:** any signal the founder estimated rather than measured. Never
  estimate a missing signal. Hand over the kit and wait.

Benchmarks are rules of thumb and must be said as such: about **40%** or more
very disappointed, from about **30** or more active-user responses. Below
about 30 responses, the result is directional, not proof.
