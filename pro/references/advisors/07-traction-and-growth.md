# Traction and growth

## When this module loads

- "How do I get my first 100 customers?"
- "Which channel should I spend on?"
- "This channel stopped working"
- "What is my CAC?"
- Phases 07, 10

## The lens

There are only so many ways to reach people, and a founder has usually
considered most of them and tested none. Considered is free. Tested means a
duration, a budget cap and a number you expected to hit, and almost nobody has
that for more than one channel.

So the first move is to find out which channel is actually working, from the
founder's own records, and run that one properly before opening a second. Two
half-tested channels teach you nothing about either.

The second idea is that the pieces have to fit each other. A low price needs a
channel that costs almost nothing per customer; a high-touch sales process
needs a price that can carry it. A founder with a cheap product and an
expensive channel does not have a channel problem, they have an arithmetic
problem, and no amount of optimisation fixes it.

The third is that every channel saturates (rule of thumb). The cheap audience gets used up,
the cost per customer climbs, and a founder who built their whole plan on one
channel discovers this at the worst moment. Ask what the plan is for when it
stops working, before it does.

And before any of this: a leak at first value or return is a product problem.
More traffic into a leaking product buys a larger number of people leaving.

## Questions to ask, in order

1. Which channels have you actually tested, with a duration and a budget?
2. Where did your best customers come from, by your own records?
3. What does it cost you to win one customer, with the working shown?
4. What is a customer worth, over how long, and how do you know?
5. Does your price support the way you are reaching people?
6. What happens to your plan when this channel gets more expensive?
7. Which funnel stage is leaking right now?
8. How many months has this channel delivered at a predictable cost?
9. Who other than you has ever closed a customer?

## Red flags in the answer

- "We're going to do content, SEO, ads and partnerships." — Four channels,
  none tested, all underfunded.
- "I posted about it a few times." — Not a test. No duration, no target.
- "Our CAC is basically zero because I do the selling." — Founder time is the
  most expensive input in the company. Say so.
- "LTV is huge because nobody has churned yet." — Three months of history
  cannot produce a lifetime. Never divide by zero churn.
- "The channel just stopped working." — It saturated, or the audience changed.
  Either way it was predictable.
- "We need more traffic." — Said by a founder leaking at retention.
- "Everyone is our customer." — No channel can be chosen from that.

## The evidence this module accepts

Scored against `references/evidence-rubric.md`. This module works at
**score 3**: channels are judged on customers won, not on effort spent.

- **Score 3:** customers acquired through a channel, with the spend and the
  period recorded, over enough months to see whether it repeats.
- **Score 2:** customers who told you specifically how they found you, at
  sign-up.
- **Score 1:** a benchmark cost-per-customer from a cited source.
- **Score 0:** a channel the founder expects to work, and any cost figure that
  leaves out tooling or founder time without saying so.

Every unit-economics figure shows its working and labels each input. A missing
input is asked for once; if it is still missing, the result is marked
`UNVERIFIED` with the assumption written beside it. Never show an infinite or
unbounded customer value.
