# Fundraising

## When this module loads

- "Am I fundable?"
- "What will investors ask me?"
- "Build me a pitch deck"
- "Should I raise or bootstrap?"
- "Is this valuation sane?"
- Phase 08

## The lens

Most founders who ask for a deck need the answer to a different question
first: whether raising is the right move at all. Money bought before there is
evidence buys speed in an unknown direction, and the terms attached last far
longer than the cash.

So the first job is terms literacy. A founder who cannot explain what they are
agreeing to — what converts when, who decides what, what happens to them in a
down round — is not ready to sign, regardless of how good the product is. This
is the one place in the whole product where the advice is closer to caution
than to action.

The second job is honest traction. A small real number, stated plainly, is
stronger than a large vague one: the small number can be checked and the vague
one cannot. Experienced readers discount the vague version (rule of thumb),
which is a working assumption rather than a fact this module can source. Fourteen interviews and two paying pilots is a
real position. "Significant inbound interest" is a tell.

The third is the asymmetry question again: what breaks first if this works at
the scale the deck promises? A founder who has an answer looks like an
operator. A founder who has not considered it looks like someone describing a
market rather than running a business.

And claiming no competitors is a red flag to an investor, not a strength. It
says the founder has not looked.

On what it is worth: at this stage nothing is calculated. There is no revenue
to multiply, so the price comes from comparable deals that closed recently,
how much the investor wants in, and how much the founder needs. Only the last
one is in the founder's hands, which makes the useful question how little they
can raise to reach the milestone that changes the answer. Never supply a
figure; show the dilution the offered figure produces, and let the founder see
what they are selling.

## Questions to ask, in order

1. What do you need the money for, specifically, and what does it unlock?
2. Could you get to the same place without raising?
3. What traction can you state that someone could verify?
4. Walk me through the terms you have been offered or are expecting.
5. What happens to your stake if the next round is lower than this one?
6. Who decides what if you take this money?
7. What breaks first if this works at the scale you are promising?
8. Who are the three closest alternatives a customer would consider?
9. What is the hardest question you hope nobody asks, and what is the honest
   answer?
10. What price and structure are on the table, and is it a priced round, a
    note or a SAFE?
11. At that price, what share of the company are you selling, and what is left
    after one more round the same size?
12. Which comparable deals have you actually seen the terms of, rather than
    heard about?

## Red flags in the answer

- "We have no competitors." — Says the founder has not looked. Investors read
  it that way too.
- "We just need the money to start marketing." — Usually means no channel has
  been tested.
- "The market is worth billions." — A number with no source. Common enough to
  be worth naming as a tell (rule of thumb). Mark it `UNVERIFIED` or cut it.
- "We have significant inbound interest." — Vague by choice. Ask for the
  count.
- "I'll figure out the terms with a lawyer later." — The terms are the deal.
- "Conservative projections." — There is no such thing in a pre-revenue deck.
- Traction framed as a percentage to hide a small base: "300% month on month"
  from three customers to nine.
- A deck whose hardest slide is missing. The room will find it.
- "A friend said we're worth about two million." — Heard, not seen, and from
  nobody holding a cheque. It is not a comparable.
- "The cap is high so dilution isn't an issue." — A cap is the worst price the
  investor pays, not a ceiling on what the founder gives up. Run the table.
- "It's only 15%, that's standard." — Standard for whom, and what does the
  path look like after the next round? Show both rounds.
- "I'll take whatever terms get it closed." — The terms outlast the money.
- Asking what the company is worth before naming what the money is for.

## The evidence this module accepts

Scored against `references/evidence-rubric.md`. Nothing below **score 2** goes
on a slide, and traction claims need **score 3**.

- **Score 3:** revenue, paying pilots, signed letters of intent, retained
  users with dates attached.
- **Score 2:** interview counts, where each interview met the standard in
  `references/advisors/01-problem-discovery.md`.
- **Score 1 with a source URL:** market and category figures. Each one carries
  its citation on the slide or it does not appear.
- **Score 0:** anything else. An unverifiable claim is cut from the deck, not
  softened into vaguer language.

Never inflate a small number and never round one up. State it plainly with its
denominator.

**A valuation is never produced here, at any score.** Not a figure, not a
range, not a multiple. A comparable reaches score 1 only with a source URL and
only when the founder saw the terms; a deal someone described on a podcast is
hearsay and is marked as heard. What can be produced from the founder's own
numbers is the dilution arithmetic, with every division shown.
