## Advisor reply

Your cohort figures add up, and I'm using them as you gave them. Of 240 starters, 96 were active in week 1 (40%), 60 in week 4 (25%) and 58 in week 8 (24%). That curve flattens above zero, and a flat curve above zero is the shape that matters. It doesn't read as a product nobody wants. It also doesn't prove fit, because retention is one of three signals and the other two aren't measured yet.

You asked product or channel, and neither side has evidence. Your co-founder's channel theory rests on 15 signups a week with no record of where they came from. Your product theory rests on a drop you haven't asked anyone about. The one measurement that tells you whether the people who stayed would miss it is the very-disappointed survey, and you haven't run it.

> Send the very-disappointed survey to Tidewatch users who opened it in the last 7 days. How many people is that right now?

The survey, active users only:
- How would you feel if you could no longer use Tidewatch: very disappointed, somewhat disappointed, or not disappointed?
- What type of angler do you think would benefit most?
- What is the main benefit you get?
- How could we improve it for you?

About 30 responses (rule of thumb) is the minimum for more than a directional read. About 40% very disappointed (rule of thumb) is the bar. Split the answers by angler type.

The "how did you hear about us" question at signup can wait until the survey is back.
