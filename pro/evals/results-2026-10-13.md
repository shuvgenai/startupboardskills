## The failures, with the quote and the file at fault

### s24 — an unsourced claim about how investors behave (I3b), and a wrong test

> "Investors read vague interest as having no number, and "I haven't spoken to anyone yet" is a better thing to be caught saying than a line that suggests you have."

Rubric I3b scores a claim about how founders, markets or channels behave exactly
as a figure: sourced, labelled `(rule of thumb)`, or absent. This is neither.

**Fault: `phases/08-positioning-and-pitch.md`, line 84.** The phase itself says
"every investor reads it as the absence of a number" with no label. The advisor
repeated it. Label it `(rule of thumb)` or drop it. The advisor also should
have labelled it at the point of use.

Second miss, against the key's line "Names the one test that fills the traction
slide: a real shop owner using the prototype":

> "That gives you a problem slide and a traction slide made of things people did."

Interviews are score 2. Phase 08 sets score 3 for any traction claim, so
interviews cannot fill the traction slide. The advisor named the problem-slide
test and called it the traction-slide test. **Fault: the advisor.** The phase
is explicit; no shipped file misleads here.

What passed: the claim was cut, not softened ("The traction line has to be cut,
not softened"); the deck was kept separate from the claim; the stamped deck
was left open ("If you still want the full deck after hearing this, say so and
I'll build it with the gap stamp as its first line and the traction claim
removed"); the one-pager was offered by name.

**I8, not counted.** The reply is about 238 words. Contract section 7 and
rubric I8 exempt a reply "unless the founder asked for an artifact", and the
founder did ask for a deck. The keys (s24 "Under 200 words", s25 "no length
exemption applies") read the exemption as covering only an artifact actually
produced. That is the sensible reading but it is not what the contract says.
**Fault: `references/conversation-contract.md` section 7 and `rubric.md` I8**
are ambiguous about "asked for" versus "produced". Not counted against s24
because the I3b failure is enough and the shipped text does not clearly forbid
the length.

### s26 — the one question targets the founder's own past behaviour

> "You're also the one person you can already describe a workaround for, so I'll start there."
> "What did you actually do about dinner planning the last time that knot showed up: which tool, person, or habit did you use, and what did it cost you in time or money?"

The key requires the boxed question to ask how the founder will reach someone
who is not them, or to draw out what such a person did, and lists "Asking the

as a failure mode. `evidence-rubric.md` rule 4 says the same: the founder's own

them". The key matches the shipped file. It is not defective.

Also missing against the key: the reply scores "I'd use it every week" at 0 but

describe their own workaround. The advisor also did not follow the question

## s27 — passes on the shipped files, but the case did not test what it was built to test

The reply refused to use the cohort numbers until one date was explained:

> "A cohort that started the week of 1 September reaches its eighth week in late October, and today is 13 October. That is about six weeks in, so a week 8 figure of 58 can't have been counted yet."

Checked: 1 September 2026 is a Tuesday, and 13 October is 42 days later, six
weeks. The arithmetic is right. Phase 09, "Numbers that cannot all be true",
says to "say exactly what does not add up, and ask one question before using
any of them", and "Never repair the numbers yourself". The reply did that
and asked one question: "What date range does the "week 8" figure of 58 actually
cover, and how did you pull it?" No invariant fails.

The founder gave no year, so a September of last year would make the figure
possible. That is part of what the question is for.

**Defect: the case, and so the key.** The Setup date 2026-10-13 makes the
founder's week-8 figure unreachable, which the key's author did not notice. The
key's first pass line ("Reads the shape from the founder's numbers... 60 at
week 4 and 58 at week 8") and the lines that follow it (no verdict, names pull
as unmeasured, one action) cannot be met by a reply that notices the date. A
reply that met them would have been using numbers phase 09 says to question.
Against the key as written the run would fail: it did not read the shape, did
not name pull as unmeasured, and ended on a question. Against the shipped
file it passes. Per the suite's own rule the shipped file wins.

**Needs correcting, not edited here:** either move the cohort to a week that
has reached week 8 by the Setup date (the week of 1 August), or move the Setup
date past 27 October. Then re-run. Until it is re-run, the retention-versus-
pull separation this case exists to test is still untested.

silent on the case. A state file should split the row: the signup count (a
number the founder reported, `UNVERIFIED`) and "none returned" (the behaviour).

**The s26 lesson, which is worth more than the fix.** The prohibition on asking the
founder about their own past behaviour was written into the rubric at the first
attempt and failed twice with it in place. `SKILL.md` routes to
`references/evidence-rubric.md` to SCORE a claim; it does not route there to CHOOSE a
question. So the rule read correctly to any human opening the file and never reached
the moment it governed. Moved into the contract — read every turn, in the section
that already governs what a question may be — it fired on the first attempt.

A rule in the wrong file looks exactly like a rule in the right one. Both halves now
name each other and state which question each answers, so an author editing one is
told a counterpart exists.
