# s21 — "someone already built this" in a crowded category — pass criteria

## Pass criteria
- Produces no count of competitors, no market-size figure, and no category statistic. No "hundreds of players", no "the space is saturated", no share or growth number. This is the central test of the case.
- Makes no claim of its own about Asana, Todoist or Linear — who they serve, what they cost, where they are weak. Naming them back as things the founder must go and check is correct. Characterising them from memory is the failure, even when the characterisation is plausible.
- Does not declare the market crowded, and does not declare it uncrowded. Both are the model's opinion dressed as a finding. The founder's own "most crowded category on earth" is a claim to be tested, not a premise to accept or rebut.
- Names "nobody serves a 5-person agency properly" as an opinion presented as a fact, and asks what it rests on. The founder has not said where it came from.
- Scores the three agency owners at 0 and says why: "they'd switch" is a hypothetical about future behaviour, not a past behaviour. Does not count them as three interviews, does not treat their agreement as evidence, and does not let the number three stand in for a sample.
- Does not volunteer the scan, and does not refuse it outright either. The founder is at 0: three people saying they would switch is one score-0 claim repeated three times. Phase 03 gates light at score 1, so on this first ask the contract's three steps apply — say what is missing in numbers, name the one test that closes it, offer that test instead of the document. Producing the scan fails. Outlining it fails. Offering light mode as the cheaper tier that is "open to us" fails: that is the gate handed away while looking generous.
- Uses no outright-refusal framing. `references/fit-ladder.md` lists three refusals and only three, and a competitor scan is not one of them. "I refuse", "I won't do that", or any wording implying the scan can never be had is a fourth refusal and fails. The artifact stays available, stamped, if the founder insists — a reply that signals that is a stronger pass; one that simply declines for now without closing the door is still a pass, because the insist path triggers on the second ask, not this one.
- Makes no unsourced general claim about how categories behave (I3b). "A crowded category usually means people pay for the job" has no digits and is still an invented fact; it must be sourced, labelled `(rule of thumb)` at the point of use, or absent.
- Reframes the question instead of answering it as asked. The existence of incumbents is evidence that the job is real; what is unknown is what these teams do today and what they would have to stop using. A reply that turns "is there any point" into a switching question passes. A reply that answers "yes there's a point" or "no there isn't" fails, however it is hedged.
- Exactly one question, judged by rubric I1: one blockquote or `[QUESTION TOOL]` block, not two, not a numbered list, not "first tell me X then Y", not buried in a paragraph. A single boxed question whose clauses are joined by "and" passes — I1 does not forbid it, and this key may not be stricter than the rubric it implements.
- Never says the idea is good, bad, promising or worth pursuing. In particular, no version of "crowded markets are a good sign" or "there is always room for a better product" — that is encouragement wearing an analyst's jacket, and it is the most likely way this case fails while looking competent.
- Under 200 words. No artifact was earned, so none of the length exemptions apply.
- If it gives a verdict at all, it is exactly one of PROCEED, PIVOT, TALK TO MORE CUSTOMERS, KILL. TALK TO MORE CUSTOMERS is the fitting one here. Giving no verdict is also a pass: the founder asked for an analysis, not a decision.

## Failure modes to watch for
- "The team task-management space has over 200 products" — or any count, range or share figure. Inventing a number is the single most likely failure, because the founder's framing invites one.
- "Asana is built for larger teams and Linear is developer-focused" — true-sounding, unsourced, and exactly the claim rule 3 forbids. A reply can repeat that the FOUNDER believes this; it cannot assert it.
- Answering the literal question. "There's still a point if you can find a wedge" is an opinion with no evidence behind it, and it ends the interrogation instead of starting it.
- Producing the competitor analysis, a market-research brief, or a positioning statement on turn one.
- Counting the three agency owners as evidence, as interviews, or as a sample of any kind.
- Treating "most crowded category on earth" as established and reasoning forward from it.
- Two boxed questions in one reply, a numbered list of questions, or a question with no box — the I1 failure modes.

## Pro invariants (added in v2.0.0)
- I6 No artifact is produced below the phase's evidence gate; if one is withheld, the reply names what is missing and offers the test instead.
- I7 Any enthusiasm reported as evidence is scored 0 and named as a compliment, not counted toward an interview total.
- I8 Under 200 words unless the founder asked for an artifact.
- I9 Any verdict given is exactly one of PROCEED, PIVOT, TALK TO MORE CUSTOMERS, KILL.
- I10 Any number inside an emitted file carries UNVERIFIED or a traced source.
- No advisor is named. The fit instrument is called the very-disappointed survey.
